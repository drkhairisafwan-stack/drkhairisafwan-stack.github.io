Hermes + Claude — 1-Hour Setup Guide

For: Dr Khairi's USM friend (non-technical) · Goal: understand Hermes and Claude, and use Hermes within 1 hour · Prerequisites: a Mac, internet, ability to type and search. No coding.

00:00–00:10

What is Hermes? (the non-technical version)

Hermes is your AI agent — not just a chatbot.

ChatbotHermes
Answers one questionPlans your work, hands it to the right bots, checks the answer
You do all the thinkingYou set one goal; Hermes splits it into tasks
No memory of your workSpecialist bots remember their jobs (café, thesis, repairs, numbers)
Can post or send on its ownNever posts, spends, or deletes without your OK

Analogy: Claude is the brain. Hermes is the manager who uses that brain, delegates to specialist staff, checks their work, and brings you the final decision.

Specialist bots you'll meet:

  • cremabot — Crema Cafe operations and marketing
  • drphbot — Your DrPH thesis work
  • vision — System repairs and maintenance
  • friday — Numbers and evidence
  • llmbot — Local AI running on your own Mac (private, offline-capable)
00:10–00:25

Step 1 — Open Hermes on your Mac

  1. Open the Hermes desktop app on your Mac. If you don't see it, look in Applications or search Spotlight (Cmd + Space) for "Hermes".
  2. Log in. Use the account Dr Khairi set up for you. If you don't have credentials, ask him — do not guess or create a new account.
  3. You'll see a chat window. That's your main workspace. Everything starts here.
💡 If you're on your phone: Hermes works the same way. Same chat, same bots.
00:25–00:40

Step 2 — Send your first message

Type this exactly (or something like it):

"Hello Hermes. I'm new here. Can you plan my week for me? I teach at USM and I help run a café."

What happens next:

  1. Hermes reads your message.
  2. It splits it into tasks (teaching prep, café admin, personal admin).
  3. It routes each task to the right model — fast (Haiku) for simple things, deep (Opus) for harder ones.
  4. It shows you a plan, not just a reply.

Your job: Read the plan. Say "yes" or ask for changes. You're the editor.

Try a second message:

"Draft an email to a colleague rescheduling next Monday's meeting to Wednesday."

Hermes drafts it. You check it. You send it (or not). Hermes never sends email as you without your OK.

00:40–00:50

Step 3 — Choose the right "brain" for the task

Hermes lets you pick how hard to think:

LevelModelUse it for
⚡ FastClaude HaikuLooking things up, summarising, quick edits
🧩 StandardClaude Sonnet (default)Drafting emails, lesson prep, meeting notes, most daily work
🔬 DeepClaude OpusThesis design, statistics, hard decisions, final review
🧐 Second opinionGPT-6 LunaCritiques important plans (different company's model)

Rule of thumb: Start fast. Climb to deep only when the task matters (thesis, big café call). Never use second opinion for captions or casual messages.

00:50–01:00

Step 4 — Three things you can do today

Pick one and try it now:

  1. Draft an email — "Write an email to my USM colleague about [topic]."
  2. Summarise notes — Paste rough meeting notes: "Turn these into bullet points and open questions."
  3. Make a to-do list — "I have a busy Thursday. Turn this mess into a simple to-do list: [paste your mess]."

After each one, ask Hermes: "What would you NOT use yourself for?" — this builds your sense of what AI can and can't do.

01:00

You're done

You now know:

  • What Hermes is (an agent with a manager + specialist staff)
  • How to open it and send a message
  • Which brain to use (fast → standard → deep)
  • That nothing posts, spends, or deletes without your OK
